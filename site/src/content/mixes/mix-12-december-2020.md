## Liner Notes

December 2020 closes the pandemic year with the moodiest, most atmospheric mix in the archive. One hundred and one minutes. Forty-three tracks. The lowest energy mean yet — 0.211. Where Mix 11 was dense and underground, Mix 12 is spacious and melancholic. The DJ who packed seventy-eight tracks into ninety-five minutes last month has now decided that forty-three in a hundred is enough. The restraint is the message.

Nora En Pure's "Enchantment" opens the mix with nearly six minutes of space before the second track arrives — the longest opening since Mix 09. Then MEDUZA's "Paradise" in a Cassian remix, and Mood II Swing's "Closer" at twelve minutes. The Mood II Swing classic has appeared in four mixes now, and its placement near the top of Mix 12 signals something: this is going to be a mix that prioritises depth over speed. "Closer" is a track that needs room to breathe, and it gets it here.

YOTTO & Cassian's "Grains" arrives at nineteen minutes, and the data suggests it gets an extraordinary ride — roughly twelve minutes of space before the next identified track. In a mix this sparse, twelve minutes with a single track is a statement of absolute confidence. "Grains" is a slow-building, hypnotic piece that rewards patience, and the DJ gives it all the time it needs. This is the antithesis of the early marathons, where a new track arrived every ninety seconds. The DJ has learned that sometimes the best thing to do is let a record play.

Fine Young Cannibals' "She Drives Me Crazy" appears in three forms across a ten-minute stretch — the Dimitri From Paris Remix and two versions of the David Z 12" Remix. It's the boldest pop interpolation since U2's "With or Without You" in Mix 06. An '80s pop-funk classic, pulled into a late-night house context, given enough room to reveal dimensions the radio version never showed. The Dimitri From Paris treatment in particular — disco-inflected, extended — transforms it from a three-minute single into a ten-minute journey. It's the centrepiece of the mix, the moment where everything slows down and the DJ's love of melody overrides any obligation to maintain intensity.

Master KG's "Jerusalema" at the 74-minute mark is THE song of 2020 — the South African gospel-house track that became a global dance challenge during lockdown. Its inclusion here, deep in a mix full of European underground selections, is both a nod to the cultural moment and a recognition of the track's genuine quality. "Jerusalema" is a house record that crossed over without compromising. In a mix made during the year that redefined isolation, its communal energy — people all over the world dancing alone to the same song — carries a weight that transcends the dancefloor.

The energy arc is front-loaded, then descends — the only mix to date where the opening quarter carries the most energy and the final quarter carries the least. It's the shape of a candle burning down. Mixed on a December night, the shape feels appropriate: the longest night of the year, the mix slowly dimming until it's barely there.

Gorgon City appear three times — "Trapdoor," "Alive" with PAX, and "Sky High" — their strongest presence in a single mix. They've become the UK house anchor in the same way that RÜFÜS DU SOL anchors the melodic side. Marc Marzenit's "Perron" returns at the 70-minute mark, deep in the mix's quietest section, now functioning almost as a lullaby.

Morgan Page's "The Longest Road" in the deadmau5 remix makes its third appearance in the archive — and it always arrives in the same structural position: not at the beginning, not at the end, but in the first third, as a threshold. Once you hear it, the mix has crossed from opening into body. It's become a gateway track, the moment the DJ signals that the preliminary pleasantries are over and the real work is beginning.

The close is a barely-there descent through Logic1000, MEDUZA's "Discopolis 2.0" remix, and David Guetta & MORTEN's "Dreams" — named, like Fleetwood Mac's track that appeared in Mix 04, with a word that carries extra weight at the end of 2020. The pandemic year ends not with a bang but with a sigh. Twelve mixes now exist. The journey from three-hour-forty-five-minute enthusiastic sprawl to a one-hour-forty-one-minute atmospheric meditation is complete. The DJ who began by including everything has learned that the spaces between the notes matter as much as the notes themselves. The pandemic forced an inward turn, and the music followed — deeper, quieter, more patient, more honest. Whatever comes next will be built on this foundation.
