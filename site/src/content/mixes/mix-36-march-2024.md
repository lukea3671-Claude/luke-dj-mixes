## Liner Notes

Nineteen tracks. Fifty-one minutes. Mix 36 has the lowest track count in the entire archive — fewer than a third of what Mix 04 contained. Where the DJ once packed 164 tracks into three and a half hours, they now need fewer than twenty to say what they mean. The journey from excess to economy reaches its most extreme expression here.

The sparseness is the defining feature. With only nineteen tracks across fifty-one minutes, each selection gets an average of nearly three minutes — luxurious by the archive's standards. The DJ is letting records breathe in a way they haven't since Mix 12's atmospheric close. Every track earns its place not by fitting into a sequence but by being the only possible choice for its moment.

Céline Dion's "The Power of Love" at fourteen minutes — the original and Cyril's remix, back to back. It's the most overtly sentimental selection in the archive: a power ballad from 1993, placed in a 2024 house mix with no ironic armour whatsoever. The DJ has been building toward this kind of fearlessness for thirty-six mixes. In Mix 01, the pop crossovers felt like someone mixing their general playlist. Here, Céline Dion feels like a deliberate artistic choice, the selection of someone who has nothing to prove.

Lane 8's "Loving You" in the Solee Remix returns at forty-four minutes — the thread that has run from Mix 01 through the entire archive. In this sparse, minimal mix, its warm melody carries even more weight than usual. It's followed by Creed's "One Last Breath" — first in a Jojo Lorenzo remix, then the original. The mix closes on a hard rock ballad about desperation and survival. The title reads as dramatic, but in the context of nineteen carefully chosen tracks across fifty-one minutes, it reads as earned.

Dennis Ferrer's "Hey Hey" in a Jack Back remix at thirty-nine minutes provides the mix's peak functional moment — one of house music's most recognisable vocal hooks, deployed at the three-quarter mark when the mix needs one last push of energy before its rock-ballad close.

Disclosure's "You & Me" in a Rivo Remix at twenty-four minutes connects back to Mix 04, where the Flume Remix appeared. The same song, different treatment, seventeen mixes apart. The archive is a web of these connections — tracks that recur in different forms, creating a fabric of reference and self-reference that only becomes visible across the full span.

Nelly Furtado's "Say It Right" at twenty-two minutes is the archive's most persistent pop selection — it has appeared in remixed form since Mix 15. Here it arrives in what appears to be something close to the original, which after years of hearing it through house filters, sounds almost radical.

Mix 36 is proof that less can be infinitely more. Nineteen tracks, no filler, every selection a statement. The quiet era has found its purest expression.
