## Liner Notes

December 2019. The last mix before the world changes. Two hours and six minutes, fifty-two tracks — the shortest and most economical mix in the archive so far. Where Mix 01 contained ninety tracks across nearly four hours, Mix 06 achieves more with less than half that count. The edit has arrived. The DJ who once couldn't stop is now someone who knows when enough is enough.

The opening tells you immediately that this is a different mix. Crazy P's "Heartbreaker" is disco-inflected dance music with a live band feel — warm, organic, inviting. It's followed by Dele Sosimi's Afrobeat, then David August's "Hamburg Is for Lovers," a track whose title alone suggests a late-night European sensibility. The pop crossovers that peppered earlier mixes are almost entirely absent. This is a DJ who has found their lane.

RÜFÜS DU SOL's presence here is subtle but significant. They appear first as remixers — Foals' "The Runner" in RÜFÜS DU SOL's rework, which plays twice in the opening fifteen minutes. This is a reversal from the earlier mixes where RÜFÜS DU SOL appeared as headliners. Now they're the lens through which other artists are refracted. "New Sky" returns near the close, followed by its Audiofly remix, creating the same double-version technique that has become a signature move. But the emphasis has shifted: RÜFÜS DU SOL are no longer the destination. They're the atmosphere.

The middle section is where Mix 06 reveals its emotional core. Robin S.'s "Luv 4 Luv" in a Stonebridge mix — classic diva house. Maxwell's "Bad Habits" remixed for the club. Jeremih's "Planez." There's an R&B warmth running through this stretch that the earlier mixes never achieved. The DJ has discovered that soulful vocals over house beats create something more emotionally resonant than instrumental electronic music alone. This thread — R&B sensibility meeting dance production — will continue to develop across the archive.

A sequence around the fifty-minute mark captures the mix's range in miniature. A dance remix of U2's "With or Without You" sits beside the original, back to back. It's audacious: the biggest rock band in the world, their most iconic ballad, dropped into a house set. But it works because by this point in the mix, the listener trusts the DJ's instincts. The mood is right. Monolink's "Take Me Home" in Purple Disco Machine's remix follows, and the transition from U2's yearning into Monolink's melodic warmth feels seamless. These are the moments where mixing becomes something more than track selection — it becomes storytelling.

The energy arc closes strong. Mix 06 is the first in the archive to finish at a higher energy than it starts — the outro quarter carries the most intensity. MEDUZA's "Lose Control," Joris Voorn's "Antigone" in YOTTO's extended mix, Lovebirds' "Want You In My Soul" — the final third builds toward something rather than away from it. It's the energy signature of resolution, of ending on a high note rather than a fade. After five mixes that wound down or plateaued, this one rises.

FKA twigs' "Two Weeks" appears at the 66-minute mark — an R&B-electronic hybrid from an artist who exists entirely outside the dance music ecosystem. It's one of the most unexpected selections in the early archive, a song built on vulnerability and vocal acrobatics placed between Regard's "Ride It" and a Queen cover. The inclusion signals something: the DJ's taste is no longer bounded by what other DJs play. The listening has expanded beyond Beatport and into the broader musical landscape.

The close is the most intentional ending yet. CHVRCHES' "Death Stranding" — named after the Hideo Kojima game — is atmospheric and melancholic. It's followed by deep house, then RÜFÜS DU SOL's "New Sky" and its remix, Flume's "Rushing Back" and "Friends," and finally an ambient track and a New Age piece. The mix dissolves from dance into pure texture. The lights come down slowly. It's the ending of a year — the last two months of 2019, before a pandemic reshapes every aspect of life, including how and why someone might sit alone and make a mix.

Six mixes in, and the arc of the first year is complete. The journey from three-hour-forty-five-minute sprawl to a two-hour focused session is the story of someone finding their voice by learning what to leave out. The track count halved. The genre palette narrowed and deepened. The endings improved. RÜFÜS DU SOL evolved from featured artists to atmospheric touchstones. The signature tracks — "That Acid Track," "Shanti," "Closer," "Children" — established their recurring roles. And most importantly, the mixes began to feel like they were going somewhere specific rather than everywhere at once. The marathon era is over. What comes next will be shaped by a world none of us saw coming.
