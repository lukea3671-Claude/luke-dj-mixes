## Liner Notes

The marathon instinct hasn't faded — two hours and forty minutes, 111 tracks — but Mix 03 is where the ear starts sharpening. The track count is the highest of the archive so far, yet the duration has dropped by a full hour from the first two mixes. The math tells its own story: shorter gaps, faster decisions, tighter curation within the sprawl. The DJ is starting to edit on the fly.

It opens with Anderholm's "Timecode" and immediately moves into RÜFÜS DU SOL's "Treat You Better" — but this time it's the Cassian remix. That distinction matters. In Mix 01, RÜFÜS DU SOL appeared in their original forms. By Mix 03, the preferred versions are the remixes — the deeper, more textured readings. The appreciation has moved beyond the song to the treatment. Cassian, who had a cameo in Mix 02, is becoming part of the vocabulary.

Fred again.. makes his first appearance in the archive at track 13 — "Danielle (smile on my face)," barely twenty-two minutes in. It's an early, relatively low-key entry for an artist who will become one of the most frequent names across eleven future mixes. There's no fanfare. He slips in between Dead or Alive's "You Spin Me Round" and an Elektrodrei track, which is exactly the kind of unexpected context that makes these mixes distinctive. A Fred again.. track framed by an '80s pop classic and German techno — that's the sound of someone mixing without a rulebook.

The Energy 52 thread begins here, and it begins boldly: "Café Del Mar" appears three times across the mix — the Tale Of Us Renaissance Remix, the Deadmau5 Remix (twice). This Balearic anthem, originally from 1993, will echo through the archive all the way to Mix 46. It functions as a talisman, a track that summons a specific feeling — sunset, open air, the precise emotion of being exactly where you're supposed to be. Its first appearance at the fifty-minute mark, in Tale Of Us's hands, transforms it into something almost devotional.

There's a moment at the eighty-four-minute mark that reveals the range of this mix. Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D Minor — Miklós Spányi's harpsichord recording — appears between a deep electronic stretch and Nora En Pure's "Come With Me." A classical piece in a DJ mix. It's either audacious or accidental, and either way it works, creating a moment of genuine surprise in a form that can easily become predictable. The organ-like textures of the harpsichord bleed into the electronic production that follows with an uncanny naturalness.

The recurring cast from the first two mixes is almost fully assembled now. Mood II Swing's "Closer" returns. Alex Dolby & Santos' "Raw Road" returns. Solee's "Shanti" returns. Kidnap's "Skin" (Rinzen Remix) returns. These aren't just repeat selections — they're building blocks of an identity. When you hear these tracks across multiple mixes, you start to understand that they're not chosen for variety but for something closer to ritual. They anchor each mix in a shared language.

MATADOR & ARTBAT's "Apollo 11" appears twice — once at the fourteen-minute mark and again at two hours eight minutes. A techno piece bookending a mix that spans pop, house, classical, and everything between. The track's title feels almost symbolic: an exploration, a launch into the unknown. These early mixes are exactly that. The destination isn't clear yet, but the trajectory is unmistakable — away from the mainstream and toward something deeper, more personal, more patient.

The final twenty minutes accelerate dramatically — track after track landing in quick succession. Fragma's "Toca's Miracle," CHVRCHES through Four Tet's lens, Virtual Self in Lane 8's remix, The Chemical Brothers in Soulwax's remix. It's the jukebox instinct from Mix 01 reasserting itself, but the selections are better. The remixers are more considered. And it closes with Lane 8's "Daya" — quiet, melodic, contemplative. The third mix, and already the endings are improving. Not a fade-out, not a final banger, but a gentle landing after a long flight. The instinct for how to close a chapter is solidifying, and it points forward to a time when every element of these mixes will be this deliberate.
