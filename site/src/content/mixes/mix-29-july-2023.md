## Liner Notes

One hundred and three minutes — the longest mix in six months, and the last one that will carry this particular warmth. The energy arc saves its strongest intensity for the final quarter, giving the entire session the feel of a slow, patient build rather than the front-loaded punches of Mix 28 and the sessions surrounding it. This is a mix that takes its time arriving, and when it finally does, it rewards every minute of patience.

The opening reaches into familiar territory: LMRT's "Open Your Mind" at 0:00, ANOTR & Abel Balder's "Relax My Eyes" at 2:00, Mary J. Blige's "Just Fine" at 3:00 — R&B warmth setting a very different tone from Mix 28's thirty-second blitz. Monolink's "Father Ocean" in Ben Böhmer's remix at 19:00 is a standout — atmospheric, unhurried, the kind of track that needs space to breathe. It gets it: several minutes of room before the next identified selection. In a mix that often compresses, this stretch of breathing room is conspicuous and deliberate.

Toni Braxton's "Un-Break My Heart" returns for the second time in the archive — and once again, three versions clustered together around the sixty-seven-minute mark. The Frankie Knuckles Franktidrama Club Mix bookending the classic radio version, exactly as in Mix 08. Some tracks don't just recur; they recur in the same pattern, as though the DJ has a ritual relationship with them. The Frankie Knuckles treatment has become a sacred object, deployed with the same reverence each time it surfaces.

Lane 8's "Loving You" in Solee's remix returns at 65:00 — its fifth appearance, now deep in the archive's DNA. It's followed by a Blaze & Bicep "Lovelee Dae" connection, creating a Lane 8-into-Bicep sequence that echoes the archive's earliest pairing of these two foundational artists. Morgan Page's "The Longest Road" at 56:00 marks its seventh appearance, now arriving with the regularity of a recurring dream. Solee's "Shanti" at 1:13:15 and X-Press 2's "Say What!" at 1:19:45 and again at 1:22:45 complete the signature cast. These tracks continue their appointed rounds, but this may be some of their final tours. The quiet phase is approaching, and many of these familiar faces will fall silent.

The classical thread reaches further back than ever: Stefan Johannes Bleicher's "Ciacona in D Major" from the Hexachordum Apollinis, composed in 1699, appears at 1:24:45. After Bach's Toccata in Mixes 03 and 05, the DJ has now reached back over three centuries for source material. These classical insertions are no longer surprising — they've become a signature, a recurring reminder that rhythm and structure predate electricity by hundreds of years.

Ace of Base's "All That She Wants" in Still Young's remix at 12:15 and Bicep's "Cazenove" at 1:29:15 frame the mix's range — '90s pop nostalgia and cutting-edge Belfast electronics, both welcome, both at home. Flume's "Rushing Back" returns at 1:25:45. Hannah Laing & RoRo's "Good Love" at 1:32:15 provides one final burst of floor energy before the descent.

The close is a quiet statement. Jorja Smith's "Blue Lights" at 1:37:30 — an R&B ballad about systemic racism and the violence of being young and Black in Britain. Sam Hunt's "Take Your Time" at 1:40:15 — a country song about patience and presence. Neither belongs in a DJ mix by any conventional measure. Both belong in this one. The DJ ends what turns out to be the penultimate chapter of the loud era with music about listening, about waiting, about being present. After this, Mix 30 will hold the line one final time. And then Mix 31 arrives, and the energy drops by 42%, and everything changes. Mix 29 is the last chapter before the quiet turn, though nobody listening could know that yet. The music was still loud. The music was about to stop being loud.
