## Liner Notes

Something changes in Mix 05. The running time drops by nearly an hour from Mix 04 — two hours and thirty-nine minutes — but the density increases. There are 109 tracks here, which means the average gap between songs has narrowed. The DJ is moving faster, more decisively. Selections appear and disappear with a confidence that the earlier mixes, for all their enthusiasm, didn't quite have.

The Four Tet thread is the defining feature. It's not that Kieran Hebden appears as a named artist — though he does, with "Anna Painting" at the 102-minute mark. It's that his influence is everywhere in the remix selections: Eric Prydz's "Opus" in Four Tet's remix, a track that gets nearly nine minutes of space in a mix where most tracks get two. Shura's "Touch" in Four Tet's remix. Todd Terje's "Jungelknugen" in Four Tet's remix. Martyn's "Glassbeadgames" in an eight-hour-at-Fabric dub. This is the mix where the DJ discovers the producer's producer, the remixer who can make any track feel like it belongs in a deeper, more considered world.

Andy Compton's "That Acid Track" appears three times — at 18:45, 21:15, and 60:15. Three times in one mix. The track appeared once in Mix 02 and is now in full rotation. It's become a tool, a comfort, a rhythmic anchor that the DJ drops whenever the mix needs to reset. No other track in the archive will match its frequency. By the time it appears for the third time here, it functions almost as a signature — the thing you play when you need to find your centre.

The energy arc is front-loaded — the highest energy comes in the opening quarter, then gradually descends. It's the first time a mix starts at its peak and works its way down, which reads as a set that begins with intent and slowly, deliberately, unwinds. The contrast with Mix 01's flat steadiness is stark. That was sustaining; this is shaping. The DJ is learning that a mix can have a trajectory.

Fade's "All I Got" in Chris Fortier's twenty-year dub appears twice — this is its second mix, and it will return in four more. It's a progressive house track with genuine weight, the kind of record that anchors a section rather than decorating it. Alongside Christian Smith & Wehbba's "Mutate" and Kölsch & Sasha's "The Lights," there's a progressive house corridor in the middle of Mix 05 that hints at where the DJ's taste is heading — away from festival anthems and toward the kind of records that DJs play for each other.

The pop layer is thinning but not gone. LMFAO's "Shots" sits between Kölsch's "Grey" and Krystal Klear's "Neutron Dance." Mark Ronson's "Don't Leave Me Lonely" appears in both Claptone and Purple Disco Machine remixes — the original is pop, but the treatments are pure house. This is the transition point: pop songs are no longer played as pop songs. They're filtered through dance music production, given new context, stripped of their chart identity. The DJ is no longer mixing their Spotify library — they're curating.

Bach's Toccata and Fugue returns, just as it did in Mix 03, appearing late in the mix at 106:45. It's becoming a recurring device — the classical intervention, the moment where the mix acknowledges that its ambitions extend beyond the dancefloor. The xx's "On Hold" in Jamie xx's remix appears twice, bookending a stretch that moves through Arsen Ramirez, Davide Mazzilli, and Pryda. Jamie xx, like Four Tet, represents a bridge between indie sensibility and dance production. The inclusion feels pointed: these are the producers who prove that electronic music and emotional depth aren't mutually exclusive.

Lane 8 closes the mix again — "Brightest Lights" with POLIÇA followed eventually by Bicep's "Just" and a trail of atmospheric pieces that dissolve into Kaosklap's "Road to Nowhere." The title of that final track reads as unintentional commentary. This is the fifth mix, and the road isn't nowhere — it's somewhere increasingly specific. The range is narrowing, the taste is crystallising, and the mixes are becoming recognisably the work of one person rather than a collection of everything one person has heard. The producer's ear is emerging. The journey from listener to DJ to curator is underway.
