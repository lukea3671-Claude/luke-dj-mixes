## Liner Notes

Fifty-eight minutes. Thirty tracks. Mix 17 is the first true sub-hour mix in the archive, and it's the most striking statement of discipline yet. Where Mix 01 needed three hours and forty-five minutes to say what it needed to say, Mix 17 needs less than one. The marathon era isn't just over — it's inconceivable. The DJ who emerges in this mix is someone who understands that a complete thought doesn't need to fill a room.

House takes the top spot in the genre breakdown for the first time — 38% of the mix, overtaking Dance for the first time in the archive's history. It's not a dramatic revolution; it's an accumulation. The gradual drift toward house that began in the pandemic mixes reaches its logical conclusion here. The DJ's centre of gravity has shifted permanently.

Bicep's "Glue" returns at the 42-minute mark — the original, not a remix, not a rework. Its last appearance was in Mix 01, where it sat at track three, seven minutes into a three-hour-forty-five-minute marathon. Now it arrives three-quarters of the way through a fifty-eight-minute set. Same track, entirely different context. In Mix 01 it was a discovery. Here it's a memory, deployed with the weight of everything that's happened in the four years between appearances. The DJ is reaching back to the beginning, and the gesture is both nostalgic and confident.

Fred again.. bookends the mix — "Kahan" at twenty minutes and "Dermot (See Yourself In My Eyes)" as the closing track. His presence at both the middle and the end anchors the mix in his specific emotional aesthetic: warmth filtered through technology, intimacy processed into beats. Closing with Fred again.. is a statement about where the DJ's heart currently lives.

The energy arc peaks heavy — the third quarter carries the highest intensity, with the opening and closing quarters noticeably quieter. It's a compact version of the classic DJ set shape: warm up, peak, cool down. But compressed into fifty-eight minutes, the transitions happen faster. There's no room for plateaus or extended journeys. Every section has to earn its time.

FKA twigs returns at seventeen minutes with "tears in the club" — her second appearance in the archive after Mix 10. Like that earlier appearance, she arrives from entirely outside the electronic music ecosystem, her R&B-art-pop hybrid finding space between house tracks without compromise. The DJ's ability to integrate these outliers has grown more natural; twigs doesn't feel like a digression anymore, she feels like part of the vocabulary.

David Morales' "Needin' U" at forty-five minutes is a moment of reverence. One of house music's all-time classics, originally released in 1998, deployed deep in a compact modern mix. Alongside it, Michael Bibi's "PARADISE" — contemporary tech-house from one of the genre's current stars. The two tracks, separated by decades, share more DNA than the calendar might suggest. The DJ understands the lineage.

The most remarkable thing about Mix 17 is what isn't here. No pop crossovers. No rock classics. No hip-hop. No ambient outros. Just house music and its close relatives, for fifty-eight uninterrupted minutes. After fifteen mixes of genre exploration, the DJ has produced their most focused work — and it happens to be their shortest. That's not a coincidence. Focus and brevity are the same discipline expressed in different dimensions. When you know exactly what you want to say, you don't need long to say it.
