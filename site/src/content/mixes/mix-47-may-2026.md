## Liner Notes

Ten months. After Mix 46, the longest silence in the archive's seven years. That mix had read like an ending and meant to — a hundred and three minutes that gathered every thread, RÜFÜS DU SOL named "the one artist who never left," the whole journey "compressed into a single session," and a closing track whose last word was, simply, love. It was a finale, and for the better part of a year it held. Then, from July 2025 until now, nothing. Mix 47 is what happens after the ending — not a postscript, not a loose end tidied away, but a return, occasioned by a Triple J Friday set: a reason worth breaking a silence for.

It opens with Bicep. Not in spirit this time — by name. "Just," first track, no easing in. To feel the weight of that you have to go back to the beginning. Bicep's "Glue" opened the very first mix in September 2018, "the track that opened a door — everything that follows walks through it." They became the gateway, the most important discovery of the early years, recurring across more than a dozen mixes. Then they thinned out: their last appearance by name was Mix 41, in October 2024, and by the Mix 46 finale they had become a ghost the notes could feel but no longer point to — present "in spirit if not in name." "Just" itself has not been heard since Mix 05, in October 2019 — six and a half years ago. So the comeback does not ease in. It throws the original door back open, with the original artist, on a track from the year it all still felt new, and returns to them again at the midpoint with "Chroma 010 Brillo." The gateway reopens, twice over.

The second track reaches back further still, into the festival fields. A mashup welds Avicii's "Levels" to Sebastian Ingrosso and Alesso — the Swedish House Mafia bloodline, the sound of 2012 main-stage euphoria. Avicii has surfaced in this archive since Mix 01, and "Levels" itself rang out as recently as Mix 30; Ingrosso threaded through Mixes 25 and 41. To answer a quiet finale by reaching for that unembarrassed, hands-in-the-air EDM is its own statement of intent: this is a set that wants the room, not the headphones.

Then, at forty-five minutes, the deepest cut of all — "New York," in the Fahlberg remix. The same track, the same remix, that arrived at the eighty-one-minute mark of Mix 46, where RÜFÜS DU SOL were called the riverbed, "always there, shaping the current even when invisible," present in every era, "a heartbeat the body produces on its own." You would never normally repeat a record from the mix immediately before. That is precisely the point. Carrying their exact track across the silence — straight out of the finale and into the comeback — is the clearest statement the archive can make that the thread was never cut. RÜFÜS arrived in Mix 01; this is their fifteenth mix; the riverbed runs under the gap too.

If RÜFÜS are the constant, Fred again.. is the bridge, and he recurs more than anyone else here — three times. "You're A Star" with Amyl and the Sniffers, "Scared" with Young Thug, "Facilita" with Caribou and MC Teteu. He has threaded this archive since Mix 03, back in 2019; Mix 22 was so saturated with him the notes simply called it "the Fred again.. mix"; he was last heard in Mix 45 on "Bleu (better with time)," a title those notes read as "an accidental commentary on the archive itself." Not one of these three tracks has appeared before. That is exactly right for a return — the connective artist comes back, but with new material, not nostalgia. Better with time.

Beneath the headline returns runs the house spine the archive has always trusted. Sonny Fodera opens the body of the set with "My Loving" — Fodera has been a fixture of this DJ's UK-house warmth since Mix 07, through the pandemic and the consolidation and out the other side. Cloonee's "How Deep Are Your Dreams" carries the tech-house thread he has held since Mix 16. Olivia Dean arrives in a Lost Kings remix; The Shapeshifters' "Lola's Theme," last heard in Mix 19, comes back in a Tripolism remix. And Gorgon City — "Run It Back," a title that could be the mix's own subtitle — were the pandemic discovery of Mix 07, the band Mix 38 actually eulogised, marking their "final appearance" before they quietly returned in 40 and 43. The archive keeps not-quite-letting-go of them, and here they are again, refusing to stay gone.

The peak-time muscle is FISHER, as it has so often been. "Rain" is his latest entry in a line that runs back to "You Little Beauty" in Mix 04 and surfaced as recently as "Stay" in Mix 45 — the reliable festival detonator. He sits in the mix's fastest stretch, where the tempo climbs through Dean Turnley's "Actin' Tough" — an Australian producer's first-ever Beatport number one — toward the 148-BPM spike of the set, all hard, bright, room-filling energy.

The vocals keep the archive's pop heart beating. Tobiahs, the Australian producer whose remixes have shaded the late mixes, reworks Whitney Houston's "I Will Always Love You" — and Whitney has been here since Mix 01's "I Wanna Dance with Somebody," reappearing only last year in a Felix Jaehn rework in Mix 45; the diva voice rebuilt by house producers is its own quiet lineage. PinkPantheress brings "Illegal," a genuinely new voice to the archive, her garage-pop continuing the unashamed crossover comfort that ran through the early mixes' Selena Gomez and Ed Sheeran. X Ambassadors' "Unsteady" in a Boehm remix does the same work from the rock side.

The boldest reach is the peak itself: Diplo's "Two Steppin'," his country crossover, released the very month of the mix. Diplo has collaborated across this archive since Mix 02 — but never in this register; the man who gave the early mixes "Electricity" and "Sun in Our Eyes" now arrives in a cowboy hat, and it lands at the set's 148-BPM apex. This DJ has always been willing to follow a record anywhere; here the willingness extends to Nashville.

Around these run the new explorers, because the archive has never stopped finding them — roughly two-thirds of every era's artists are first-timers, and Mix 47 holds the line. JIALING's "Horny Freaks," a Brooklyn producer's own VIP edit; Łaszewo reworked by Nic Vans; Ella Langley's "Choosin' Texas"; jigitz and Charlotte Plank's "Car Crash"; Layton Giordani — last seen on a techno remix in Mix 37 — holding "Hold It Down." The set lands on that current, contemporary edge rather than a backward glance.

The shape tells the rest. Sixty-five minutes, twenty-seven tracks — tighter than the early marathons, in the disciplined register the archive earned across its quiet years, but the energy has climbed back to 0.23, up from the 0.15 of the quiet turn and near the level of the early peaks. A 120-BPM Bicep open, a long build through the 130s, a 148 spike around "Two Steppin'," then a settle back into the low 130s to land. House at the core, a third of the set, but the old breadth holds intact: techno, tech house, pop, a country-EDM remix, a drum-edged closer.

Mix 46 ended the story on love. Mix 47 answers it — the same hands, the same ear for the thread that runs between records, the gateway thrown open again and the riverbed still running underneath. The archive was never a tomb. It was waiting for a Friday worth coming back for.
