## Liner Notes

Mix 42 opens with Charlie Jeer's "Her Eyes" — a piece of ambient electronic that sets the temperature before a single beat has dropped. The energy ticks up to 0.16, barely perceptible as a change, but the tracklist carries a weight and atmosphere that distinguishes it from the mixes on either side. Thirty-one tracks in sixty-three minutes. The key of F. A mix that sounds like late November feels.

Andy Compton's "That Acid Track" appears at six minutes, and this is the last time it will be heard in the archive. Sixteen mixes since Mix 02 — the most-played track in the entire collection, a deep house constant that has outlasted eras, survived the quiet turn, persisted through every shift in taste and tempo. Its final appearance comes early, almost casually, sandwiched between Carlita's "Forever Baby" and Duke Dumont's "All My Life." There's no ceremony. The DJ doesn't mark endings; they simply move on to the next record.

The Carlita and Janet Planet double — "Forever Baby" at four minutes and again at six — flanks the Compton track like parentheses around a sentence. It's a structural choice that gives the acid track its own pocket of air, a moment of stillness in the early minutes before the mix commits to its direction.

The Maria Maria sequence at eleven minutes is the mix's centrepiece. TECH IT DEEP and Gordo's version transitions directly into Santana's original in a Casa Mata remix. The melody is unmistakable — Carlos Santana's guitar has been the emotional signature of that song for twenty-five years — and hearing it migrate from a tech-house production back to something closer to its Latin roots in the space of six minutes is the kind of journey the DJ excels at now. Not genre tourism but genre archaeology, digging through layers to find where a feeling originates.

Adam Beyer's "Don't Go" at twenty-seven minutes is the only track in the mix that could be described as heavy techno. It sits alone in the tracklist, surrounded by dance and electronic selections of a gentler disposition, and its isolation amplifies its impact. The DJ has learned that one moment of intensity in a sea of restraint hits harder than a full hour at peak energy. Mix 01 was all peak. Mix 42 knows that the valleys make the peaks possible.

Nora En Pure's "Vigilant" at thirty-nine minutes provides the mix's most serene passage. Her melodic house has the quality of sunlight through water — warm, diffused, moving without effort. Placed in the final third of a November mix, it reads as an antidote to the season's darkness, a deliberate injection of light before the mix descends toward its close.

The final quarter moves through increasingly unexpected territory. Lana Del Rey's voice appears at forty-two minutes, courtesy of a Gravagerz remix of Quavo's "Tough." Hip-hop continues its presence in the archive at fifty minutes with Zobocopy's "Grow Down." Ambient arrives at fifty-one with YAVINSOUND's "Listen To Me Now." The genre walls haven't just thinned — they've dissolved entirely.

Josiah and the Bonnevilles' "Ghost" closes the mix at sixty-one minutes. Folk music. A guitar, a voice, and a song about haunting. The archive's closing tracks have traced their own arc — from dance-floor peaks in the early years to increasingly introspective selections in the quiet era. Closing on folk is the most radical departure yet, but by Mix 42 it doesn't feel radical. It feels inevitable, as though the DJ has been walking toward this kind of honesty for the entire journey.
