## Liner Notes

Mix 22 is the Fred again.. mix. Five tracks across the ninety-one minutes — "Jungle" (Adriatique Remix), "Marea (We've Lost Dancing)," "Turn On The Lights again.." with Swedish House Mafia, "Danielle (smile on my face)," and "Tanya (Maybe Life)" — the most concentrated Fred presence in the archive. This is the moment where a recurring favourite becomes a defining influence. The mix doesn't just feature Fred again..; it's shaped by his aesthetic.

The building energy arc peaks in the second quarter — 0.273, the highest single-quarter energy in the archive since Mix 09. The mix opens modestly, builds to a sustained peak through its middle sections, and then descends through a long, atmospheric close. The shape is classic, but the execution is the DJ's most sophisticated yet.

Mood II Swing's "Closer" opens at forty-five seconds — its fifth appearance, but its earliest placement ever. Where previously it arrived mid-mix as an anchor, here it's the second sound the listener hears. It's a promotion: from supporting player to opening act. The Deepshakerz Rework's warm vocal sets the emotional register for everything that follows.

RÜFÜS DU SOL appear twice — "Innerbloom" at thirty-three minutes and "On My Knees" (Adriatique Remix) at eighty-one minutes. "Innerbloom" is the track that defined their presence in the early archive, and its return after a long absence carries the weight of nostalgia. The Adriatique remix of "On My Knees" positions them in the deep, European melodic space they've always occupied in this DJ's imagination. Two appearances, thirty minutes of music between them, both functioning as emotional way-stations in a journey that runs from house to electronic to atmosphere.

The recurring cast assembles: Marc Marzenit's "Perron" at twenty minutes, Demarkus Lewis' "Body Tonic" at sixty-one minutes, Solee's "Shanti" at sixty-two, Rui Da Silva's "Touch Me" at sixty-six, Fabio Fuso's "Obscurity" at eighty-eight. Five signatures in one mix. The DJ's toolbox is fully stocked, and they know exactly when to reach for each instrument.

Rihanna's "Skin" at fifty-seven minutes is a rare R&B-pop deep cut — not a single, not a hit, but a slow-burning album track. It's followed immediately by nestor's "Skin" — same title, different song, different genre. The double-skin sequence creates a bridge between pop sensuality and house rhythm, the vocal intimacy of one bleeding into the rhythmic pulse of the other.

The closing section is one of the archive's most carefully constructed descents. From Elderbrook & Tourist's "Howl" at sixty-three minutes, the mix moves through OFFAIAH, Tripmastaz, Grant Nelson, and David Guetta into RÜFÜS DU SOL's Adriatique Remix, Sultan + Shepard, Digital Department's "Disconnection" (in both chillout and house versions), and Julian Wassermann's "Sol." The final ten minutes slow to near-ambient tempo, with Alain Grasselli's "Sonnenschiff" providing the penultimate moment of warmth before Digital Department returns. The lights go down in graduated stages. It's the most cinematic ending since Mix 06.

House reaches 27% — its second-highest share in the archive. Combined with Dance's 43%, the core dance music genres account for 70% of the mix. The pop, rock, and hip-hop crossovers of the early archive are almost entirely absent. This is a DJ who has found their home and is decorating every room.
