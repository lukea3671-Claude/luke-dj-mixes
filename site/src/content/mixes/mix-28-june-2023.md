## Liner Notes

Fifty-seven minutes. The second sub-hour mix in the archive, and the most front-loaded energy since Mix 20. The opening quarter blazes at 0.289 — the highest single-quarter energy reading in the entire catalogue — before dropping steadily through to the close. Mix 28 arrives fast, hits hard, and gets out. It's a mix that knows exactly what it has to say and refuses to add a word more.

The opening salvo is extraordinary: "Rumble" (Skrillex, Fred again.. & Flowdan), "Danielle" (Fred again..), "Leave Me Like This" (Skrillex & Bobby Raps) — three tracks in thirty seconds. The Fred again.. and Skrillex axis dominates the first minute, establishing a baseline of kinetic energy that the rest of the mix spends its runtime gradually releasing. After the contemplative meditation of Mix 25, this is a cold shower.

Taylor Swift's "Love Story" appears in three versions between the 17-minute and 18-minute marks — the Digital Dog Remix, its radio edit, and a BlackC4t rework. Three interpretations of a pop-country crossover from 2008, compressed into ninety seconds of a 2023 house mix. It's the most audacious pop inclusion since the early archive's Drake experiments, and it works because the mix's energy is so high by this point that nothing feels out of place. The dancefloor devours everything.

Paperclip People's "Throw" returns immediately after the Taylor Swift sequence — its fifth appearance, and its placement here, after three versions of "Love Story," creates an almost comical tonal shift from pop sweetness to Detroit techno aggression. The DJ leans into the contrast. The mix's short runtime means there's no room for gentle transitions; the listener gets whiplash, and that's the point.

The BPM holds at 129.2 — the faster tempo that first appeared in Mix 23 and is becoming increasingly frequent. Combined with the front-loaded energy, it creates a mix that feels like it's racing toward something. John Summit's "Make Me Feel" and Mau P's "Your Mind Is Dirty" in quick succession confirm the tech-house direction. Gigi D'Agostino's "Bla Bla Bla" returns — always deployed as pure energy, a track whose melody is so embedded in European dance culture that it functions as a communal anthem.

Duke Dumont's "The Chant" appears twice near the close — at forty-two and forty-five minutes. A newer track from one of the archive's most consistent artists, deployed in the same double-play technique that has characterised the DJ's relationship with their favourite records throughout. Sébastien Léger's "Hutchula" follows, its deep house groove providing a brief respite before the mix's final gesture.

Miley Cyrus's "Flowers" closes the mix. The biggest pop song of 2023, played as the last thing the listener hears. It's classified as Electronic here, suggesting a remix or edit rather than the original, but the choice is significant regardless. After fifty-seven minutes of driving house, tech-house, and techno, the DJ ends with Miley. It's the same instinct that closed Mix 25 with Donna Lewis — the willingness to end with pure pop, no armour, no ironic distance. When you're confident in who you are, you can end however you want.
